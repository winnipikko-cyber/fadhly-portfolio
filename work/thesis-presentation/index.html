BAD FAITH

Case study

Make an academic argument presentable without flattening it.

01 · Context / problem

The challenge

The research connects Sartre's bad faith, digital infidelity, empirical context, methodology, rationalization patterns, and a final synthesis. A conventional slide deck could easily make that sequence feel fragmented, text-heavy, and hard to navigate during questioning.

02 · What I did

The approach

I treated the defense as an interface. The argument was divided into fifteen scenes with a consistent visual grammar for definitions, evidence, flows, questions, concept maps, findings, and synthesis. The same content can switch between a slide-native presentation mode and a continuous reading mode.

03 · Interaction decisions

Built for the room and for the phone.

  • Keyboard and button navigation for controlled presentation pacing
  • Overview navigation for jumping directly to a scene during discussion
  • Presentation mode and reading mode for different viewing contexts
  • Responsive scene-specific tuning rather than simply shrinking desktop slides
  • Charts, flows, concept maps, and visual contrasts to carry the argument instead of decorative graphics

04 · Product outcome

The thesis became a navigable research interface.

The final build works as both a live defense tool and a readable artifact after the presentation. It is deliberately no-indexed as an academic presentation rather than positioned as a public content site.
