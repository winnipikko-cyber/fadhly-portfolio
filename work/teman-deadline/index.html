TEMAN DEADLINE

Case study

Turn “tolong tugas gue” into an operable request.

01 · Context / problem

The challenge

Deadline-service requests usually arrive messy. The student may only say they do not understand a subject, omit the exact deadline, forget supporting files, or mix urgency with the actual scope. That forces the operator to conduct the same clarification chat over and over before any work can even be assessed.

02 · What I did

The approach

I designed the bot as an intake layer rather than a chat gimmick. It progressively asks for the user segment, urgency, exact deadline, request detail, and supporting material before generating an order reference. The goal is to move ambiguity out of the operator's inbox and into a repeatable product flow.

03 · Key decisions

What shaped the system

  • Separate SMA and university pathways so the service catalogue can stay relevant
  • Urgency selection before detailed briefing, so priority is explicit instead of guessed
  • Exact-deadline capture after urgency to prevent vague labels such as “urgent” from becoming the only scheduling signal
  • File and reference intake for PDFs, photos, rubrics, drafts, CVs, job descriptions, and supporting material
  • Order IDs and an admin workflow so requests can move from chat into operations

04 · Product outcome

A service conversation with a system behind it.

The useful part is not Telegram itself. The product turns an unstructured conversation into a consistent operational object: who the request is for, what is needed, when it is due, what evidence is attached, and what the operator should do next.
